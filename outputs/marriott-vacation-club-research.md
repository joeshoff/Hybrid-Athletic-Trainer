# Marriott Vacation Club: What Owners Actually Say

*Researched 2026-10-01. Numbers are 2026 unless noted.*

## Bottom line

Never buy from the developer. Developer points cost about $15–18.50 each, and the same points sell on the resale market for about $2–2.30. You lose roughly 85–90% the moment you sign. Ownership makes sense in one case: you would book multi-bedroom Marriott villas every year anyway, you buy resale, and you can live with fees that rise forever and are hard to stop paying. Outside that case, renting the same villa costs less and leaves you free to walk away.

**Worked example, 2,500 points** (roughly a week in a 2-bedroom at a mid-demand resort, more at peak):

- Developer: ~$37,000–$46,000 up front. Resale: ~$13,000–$18,000 all-in.
- Annual carrying cost: 2,500 × $0.8148 + $255 = **~$2,290/yr**, every year, rising.
- Add opportunity cost at 5%: developer buyer is really paying ~$4,300/yr; resale buyer ~$3,000/yr.
- Consumer Reports' general timeshare analysis: owning costs up to ~$2,500/yr more than renting for the first seven years. Owning only pulls ahead in year 8, and the cumulative cost doesn't even out until around year 12. That math assumes developer-style financing and closing costs. Resale shortens it a lot.

The 2026 flat fee came from a new parking charge on renters and non-owners. Treat it as a one-year reprieve. Fees went up 15% in a single year just before it.

## What owners like

**The product is good.** Owners who are happy almost always say the same thing: consistent Marriott quality, full kitchens, washer/dryer, separate bedrooms. For a large family (or grandkids) that's the real value. A 2- or 3-bedroom villa replaces two or three hotel rooms.

**The points system is more flexible than old fixed-week timeshares.** You can change resort, unit size, length of stay and dates year to year. You can bank points into next year or borrow from it.

**Resale buyers get nearly the same product.** Up to Chairman's Club (under 30,000 points), resale Abound points carry the same booking rights and benefit levels as developer points once Marriott's ~$3/pt fee is paid. That's why experienced owners on TUG and RedWeek push resale so hard.

**Hotel access improved in 2025.** Since June 2025, Abound members can book 8,000+ Marriott-family hotels directly with Club Points through a third-party platform. That makes points usable in cities where there's no villa resort.

**It's heritable.** The MVC Trust is structured as deeded Florida real estate and can pass to heirs for the life of the trust. (Some owners see that as a downside. See below.)

## What owners complain about

**Depreciation.** This is the core complaint and the numbers back it. Developer price ~$16/pt, resale ~$2/pt. The developer sales presentation is what makes the purchase look rational. Owners on Tripadvisor's long "Beware – Marriott Vacation Club Points – A Bad Deal" thread and on Trustpilot/BBB describe it as one of their worst financial decisions.

**Fees that only go up and never end.** Maintenance fees plus club dues plus exchange fees rise over time, sometimes faster than inflation. Fees run until you sell or give the ownership away, and some owners note their annual fees alone exceed what a non-owner pays to book the same resort for a comparable stay. Heirs inherit the obligation along with the points.

**Special assessments.** Annual fees cover the normal insurance deductible, but not the catastrophic deductible, which is a percentage of the property's value per event. The South Carolina resorts (Hilton Head, Myrtle Beach) have been hit with repeated storm assessments. Maui Ocean Club fees jumped 9.8% in 2024.

**Availability at peak times.** Owners report being unable to book desirable resorts in desirable weeks even months ahead. A recurring complaint: the vacations shown in the sales presentation need more points than the package they were sold.

**Sales tactics.** BBB and Trustpilot complaints describe misrepresentation, for example being told upgrading would lock in maintenance fees or that fees would spike without an upgrade. Those owners say neither turned out to be true.

**Resale buyers are now second-class at the top.** The 2026 tier changes add Reserve (30,000–49,999 points) and Pinnacle (50,000+). Only developer-purchased points count toward them. This is the first time MVC has restricted resale points. Complimentary Bonvoy elite status also doesn't come with a resale-only purchase. Rollout runs summer 2026 through Q1 2027. Owners on TUG read this as a signal that more resale restrictions may come later.

**Marriott can intercept your resale.** Marriott holds a right of first refusal on all points sales and nearly all legacy weeks, with 15–30 days to match the price. Brokers say it uses ROFR to buy up cheap listings that would otherwise pull down demand for its own sales.

**Converting to Bonvoy is a bad deal.** As of May 2026 the rate is 1 Club Point to 45–75 Bonvoy points depending on tier. Bonvoy points are worth about 0.7¢, so each converted Bonvoy point costs more than double what it's worth. Booking hotels directly with Club Points beats converting.

**The "it's real estate" argument went to court and Marriott won.** *Lennen v. Marriott Ownership Resorts* (filed 2016) argued that trust points are a membership dressed up as real property using recorded deeds. The district court dismissed it (partly as a shotgun pleading), and the Eleventh Circuit affirmed in December 2021, holding the structure doesn't violate Florida property law. So the structure is legal. The financial criticism survives the ruling: the resale market prices these points like a prepaid membership with perpetual dues.

## How to decide

- **If you won't book a Marriott villa at least once a year for the next 10+ years**, don't buy. Rent the villa from Marriott or an owner on RedWeek.
- **If you would**, buy resale. Price the deal at ~$5–7/pt all-in, not the asking price.
- **If you're shown a developer price**, divide it by the current resale price per point. That ratio is what you're paying for the presentation.
- **If you want the Reserve/Pinnacle tiers or Bonvoy elite status**, those now require developer points. That's a 30,000+ point developer purchase (~$450,000+ at Trust pricing). The perks don't justify that spend for almost anyone.
- **Budget the annual fees as rising forever.** Check the specific home resort's special-assessment history before buying weeks tied to coastal properties.
